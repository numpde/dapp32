Service payment channels
========================

Design record, 2026-10-15. This describes an intended direction and a proposed
payment flow, not an implemented contract or a complete protocol specification.
The purpose is to fund repeated API purchases without an onchain transaction
for every request, while keeping each provider's backing separate.

Actors and agreed boundaries
---------------------------

E is the onchain deposit contract. Its developer/admin maintains the catalog
of approved service providers; users do not add providers to that catalog.
Users choose a provider and fund a channel for that provider. One E deployment
can hold many channels for many users and providers. A is a channel's payment
recipient, initially an ordinary address. Making A a contract needs a concrete
purpose and is not required by this design.

Each provider has a stable providerId distinct from recipient A. Both are
fixed channel terms, bound by vouchers through the channel identity. Separate
services can therefore share one treasury address while remaining independently
catalogued providers.

The admin may add its own providers, but that gives it no right to other
channels' funds. Removing a provider from the catalog blocks only new channels
for that provider. Existing valid claims and withdrawal rights survive removal;
those operations must not depend on the catalog's current enabled flag. An
existing channel's payer, providerId, recipient, asset and authorization terms
are immutable: the admin cannot redirect its balance or suspend valid claims
through the catalog.

Direct funding of a provider-specific channel is the proposed starting point.
A general free balance in E, later allocated to channels, is an alternative,
not a committed requirement. The asset is undecided. Illustrations such as a
$50 deposit or a $0.50 API purchase express prices, not an assumption that the
contract uses native currency or fixes a native-token exchange rate.

Proposed purchase and settlement flow
------------------------------------

1. The user opens and funds a channel through an onchain transaction. That
   transaction authorizes the deposit and fixed channel terms.
2. The API returns an invoice for the requested service. The user separately
   signs an offchain voucher authorizing an increased cumulative payment to
   this channel's recipient. That authorization must bind the selected chain,
   E deployment, and channel identity/fixed terms; the signing format remains
   open. Funding E alone does not authorize arbitrary service charges.
3. Before serving, the provider verifies the voucher signature and channel
   identity, the invoice/request association, payment coverage against tracked
   obligations, and sufficient confirmed channel backing. The concurrent
   accounting rules for these checks still need design. It records the
   accepted voucher and purchase status durably, then serves without waiting
   for that voucher's onchain settlement.
4. The provider later submits the highest valid cumulative authorization it
   holds, regardless of voucher arrival order. In the proposed model, let C
   be the authorized cumulative amount and P the cumulative amount E has paid.
   C <= P produces no additional payment. For C > P, a valid claim pays only
   C - P to A and advances P to C, provided the remaining channel balance
   covers the delta. Requests do not each become independent claims for their
   full cumulative amount.

Purchases within one channel must support parallel requests. A higher voucher
authorizes its entire unpaid cumulative delta, including amounts for lower
pending or abandoned purchases if included in that total. The accounting
protocol must define safe inclusion and reconciliation of those amounts,
backing reservations, voucher ordering, retries and abandoned requests. These
mechanisms remain open; selecting parallel purchases does not solve them.

Replay protection must hold for the channel's lifetime: never reset cumulative
paid accounting while old vouchers can match the same reusable identity.
Fresh identifiers/epochs or retained monotonic accounting are alternatives;
the reuse mechanism remains undecided.

Voucher validity and visible backing alone are not an unconditional payment
guarantee. The provider must track accepted obligations and pending claims,
monitor withdrawals/closing, claim within the permitted window, and choose
confirmation and reorg policies. Provider-specific backing avoids competing
claims from unrelated providers against a shared free balance; it does not
remove those operational duties.

Proposed lifecycle and service recovery
--------------------------------------

Closing ends one funded channel so the user can recover its unclaimed
remainder. The lifecycle sketch is open -> closing -> closed. The user can
request unilateral closing through a transaction to E. The claim period starts
when E transitions to closing. Its duration is fixed at channel opening and
cannot change retroactively; the duration to use is still undecided. The
provider/API stops accepting new purchases when it observes closing under its
confirmation policy. Observation lag reduces the time remaining to claim.

During that period, signed vouchers remain claimable subject to E's validity
and deadline rules. This design supplies E with no proof of API acceptance,
service delivery, or signature creation time; the provider tracks acceptance
offchain. After the claim period, the user can withdraw the unclaimed remainder
and close the channel. Exact transition rules
remain undecided. Cooperative early close/refund may be useful, but its required
signatures and amounts have not been settled.

Payment authorization does not prove service delivery. Even binding a voucher
to a purchase hash establishes an association, not that the service was
provided correctly. The API needs an idempotent, recoverable purchase workflow:
retries should recover the same purchase and recorded provisioning outcome,
not silently create another charge or resource. External provisioning cannot
be promised globally exactly once. Recovery after partial failure, and when
failed delivery warrants a refund, still need explicit rules.

Decisions still needed
---------------------

Before implementation, settle the payment asset and amount units; voucher
signer identity and key lifecycle; invoice schema and authentication; exact
request binding and the accounting protocol for parallel purchases; claim
period length, exact closing transitions and cooperative close rules; channel
reuse versus fresh identifiers/epochs; and provisioning recovery/refund
behavior. These are gaps in the current design, not additional features
already selected.

Related patterns, not adopted specifications
--------------------------------------------

x402's EVM batch-settlement specification describes prefunded channels,
cumulative signed vouchers and periodic claims. It separates claim accounting
from a later recipient transfer. Its immutable channel configuration and
withdrawal delay are relevant patterns; its token methods, authorizer roles
and full settlement protocol have not been adopted here.
https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/batch-settlement/scheme_batch_settlement_evm.md

Raiden's service-contract specification describes a shared UserDeposit balance
and OneToN IOUs for service payments. It explicitly accepts competition for
that shared backing and uses delayed withdrawal to limit risk. That differs
from the proposed provider-specific backing here; it is not evidence that a
shared deposit guarantees all outstanding provider payments.
https://github.com/raiden-network/spec/blob/master/service_contracts.rst
