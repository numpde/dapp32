Service payment channels
========================

Design record, 2026-10-15. This is an intended design, not an implemented
contract or complete protocol. A customer directly funds a native-currency
channel with a settlement service Y, then authorizes API purchases offchain.
Y coordinates payments for multiple APIs. Collection need not precede service
provisioning. The deployment chain has not been selected.

Actors and trust boundaries
--------------------------

E is the deposit/settlement contract. The deployer/admin defines channels and
maintains the catalog of settlement services, not individual APIs. Each channel
has a stable serviceId identifying its consumer Y separately from payment
recipient A. Independently catalogued services can share a recipient. Channel
definitions persist separately from each customer's funded position.

The agent is the customer; the customer account signs vouchers. There is no
separate human-owner versus agent-signing-key role. Anyone can submit a valid
customer-signed voucher. Every collection pays the channel's current payee A,
which may be a wallet or contract. The admin can change A, including for
existing vouchers. This redirect power is an explicit trust assumption:
customer authorization does not fix a historical payment destination. The
admin still cannot fabricate the customer's payment authorization.

The admin fixes the channel's claim-period duration irreversibly when defining
it. Channel identity, serviceId, native asset and authorization rules are fixed;
payee is deliberately changeable. Customer balance, lifetime paid total and
release status belong to that customer's position, not the channel definition.
Catalog removal blocks new participation only. Existing collection and release
rights survive removal and cannot depend on the catalog's enabled flag.

One E can hold many customer/channel positions. Backing for Y is separate from
backing for Yprime; APIs using the same Y share its channel balance. E needs
neither individual API catalog entries nor per-API allocations. API credits
are Y's obligations, not E-enforceable claims. APIs need no mutual trust but
rely on Y's accounting and onward payouts. A malicious Y can misaccount API
credits; APIs bear that risk.

What E offers a channel
-----------------------

- Direct funding and top-ups in native currency, accounted in native base
  units. A reserved null channel per customer uses the same balance, movement
  and release rules; its only restriction is that it cannot have a consumer.
  It is not a separate unallocated-balance ledger or a separate lifecycle.
  No special zero claim period or automatic release bypass is selected.
- Movements debit one customer position and credit another. Moving funds out
  of a source position, like withdrawal, must respect its release waiting
  rule; an open funded channel cannot be drained arbitrarily. Exact storage
  layout and public APIs remain unspecified.
- Partial cumulative collection. Let C be a valid voucher's authorized total,
  P this customer's lifetime cumulative amount already paid in this channel,
  and B its available balance. For C > P, collection transfers min(C - P, B)
  to the current payee and increases P only by the amount transferred. The
  same voucher can collect the unpaid remainder after further funding. For
  C <= P, a valid repeated/older voucher succeeds with no payment.
- Persistent authorization and accounting. Vouchers have no expiry. Paid
  total P never resets; there are no epochs or closing/reopening resets.
  Release does not invalidate unpaid authorization: after release and later
  redeposit, an old unpaid voucher can collect against the new balance.
- Release of the entire balance. The customer requests release, starting the
  channel's fixed waiting period. After waiting, movement/withdrawal takes
  the full remaining balance at execution, including later top-ups. Top-ups
  are allowed during the wait and do not restart its deadline; collections
  can reduce the eventual remainder. The claim period delays release, not
  voucher expiration or the end of collection rights.
- State and events for funding, amounts paid and release progress. E knows
  onchain funding and collection, not all outstanding vouchers or offchain
  reservations. Onchain balance alone is not the customer's spendable amount.

What the customer does
----------------------

The customer discovers channel terms, chooses Y, directly funds its position
and tops it up as needed. It can inspect balances, cumulative paid amounts and
release status. Customer and Y retain outstanding authorizations/reservations
so their displayed spendable amount accounts for obligations invisible to E.
Dollar illustrations such as $50 funding or a $0.50 purchase do not define a
native-token exchange rate.

The funding transaction and the signed voucher are separate authorizations.
Depositing alone grants no arbitrary charge authority. Vouchers bind the
selected chain, E deployment, customer and persistent channel identity; their
payments follow the current payee, not a payee address frozen in the signature.
The customer can request release without Y's cooperation, then move or withdraw
the full remaining balance after waiting. This does not cancel old unpaid
vouchers, and the channel definition and paid total persist.

Concurrent purchases and settlement-service duties
-------------------------------------------------

Purchases for multiple APIs through Y must run concurrently within one channel.
Y verifies customer authorization, channel and invoice/request association,
checks backing against tracked obligations, and atomically accounts for
reservations, debits and API credits without overcommitting funds. Y may briefly
order ledger updates without serializing all purchases. The exact cumulative
authorization, reservation and retry protocol remains open.

A higher voucher authorizes its entire unpaid cumulative delta, including
pending or abandoned purchases whose amounts it includes. Safe inclusion,
reconciliation, voucher ordering and abandonment handling still need design.
Y durably records accepted authorization and purchase status before API
provisioning. Retries must recover the same purchase and recorded outcome
rather than silently create another charge/resource. Globally exactly-once
external effects are not promised. Neither vouchers nor purchase hashes prove
correct delivery; provisioning recovery and refunds remain open.

Y stops accepting new purchases when it observes a release request under its
confirmation policy. The waiting period starts at E's request transition;
observation lag reduces the time before the customer can release funds. Y must
monitor release, outstanding obligations, current payee and confirmations,
and collect in time to reduce the release remainder. Missing that opportunity
does not expire a voucher, but later collection depends on available backing.
Valid signatures and visible backing alone are not an unconditional payment
guarantee. E has no proof of API acceptance, delivery or signature creation
time; it enforces customer/channel settlement rules, not Y's API liabilities.

Decisions still needed
---------------------

Settle the deployment chain; signature encoding and contract-wallet
verification; invoice authentication/request binding; Y's concurrent cumulative
authorization, reservation, recovery and onward payout mechanisms; exact
release timestamp boundaries and request lifecycle, including cancellation or
repeated requests; and provisioning recovery/refunds. Channel definition,
customer position storage and exact APIs have not been chosen. These open
mechanisms do not change the selected direct-funding, null-channel, persistent
voucher/accounting, partial-collection and full-balance release rules above.

Contract precedents and related patterns
---------------------------------------

Pinned source/specification snapshots and their provenance are under
dapps/pay-to-play/references/service-payment-channels/. These are research
material, not installed dependencies or executable fixtures; third-party
imports are not fully vendored. provenance.json records exact upstream commits
and byte hashes.

E's core is a unidirectional payment-channel kernel: customer authorization
lets one settlement counterpart collect from dedicated backing. Evaluate
existing implementations of that shape before writing a new kernel. Map the
payee role to Y, with A as its payment destination. This design additionally
requires the admin's Y catalog, serviceId distinct from payout address, a
claim-period duration fixed per channel, and CAM workflows. These precedents
do not choose a dependency, deployment chain or license, or establish safety
or suitability for this design.

AbstractStreamChannel is an ERC-20 precedent, while this design selects native
currency. Its operations include open, topUp, settle, requestClose, withdraw,
close and getChannel. Its EIP-712
cumulative vouchers let the payee call settle to collect the unpaid delta
without closing the channel. Its channel identity binds payer, payee, token,
salt, authorized signer, contract address and chain ID. It has a fixed
15-minute CLOSE_GRACE_PERIOD, not a per-channel duration, and no service
catalog or separate stable serviceId. This is a source-level precedent, not
a security assessment or an assertion of audit status.
https://github.com/Abstract-Foundation/abstract-packages/blob/main/packages/contracts/src/AbstractStreamChannel.sol

The historical RaidenMicroTransferChannels (microRaiden) design targets API
and content micropayments using deposits, signed cumulative balance proofs,
top-ups, cooperative close and unilateral close with a challenge period.
Settlement is primarily at channel closure rather than periodic delta claims
while open. Its repository was archived on 2022-05-13; it is a historical
design reference, not a selected dependency.
https://microraiden.readthedocs.io/en/latest/introduction/introduction.html
https://github.com/raiden-network/microraiden

x402's EVM batch-settlement specification describes prefunded channels,
cumulative vouchers and periodic claims, separating claim accounting from
recipient transfer. Its signed maxClaimableAmount is a ceiling: the server
tracks actual charges within it. That requires trust in charge accounting and
differs from this note's cumulative entitlement C, collectible in partial
deltas as backing permits. Its receiver is a useful settlement-counterpart
analogy; the full protocol has not been adopted here.
https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/batch-settlement/scheme_batch_settlement_evm.md

Raiden's UserDeposit/OneToN design accepts competition among service payments
for shared backing and uses delayed withdrawal to limit risk. Shared-credit
risk is comparable at Y's accounting layer here; this does not imply unrelated
settlement services can drain the same E channel. Neither pattern guarantees
all API liabilities will be paid.
https://github.com/raiden-network/spec/blob/master/service_contracts.rst
