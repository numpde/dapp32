Service payment channels
========================

Design record, 2026-10-15. This is an intended design, not an implemented
contract or complete protocol. A customer funds an onchain channel with a
settlement service Y, then authorizes API purchases offchain. Y coordinates
payments for multiple APIs and later collects from the channel. Each purchase
need not wait for an onchain settlement transaction.

Actors and trust boundaries
--------------------------

E is the deposit contract. Its developer/admin maintains a catalog of approved
settlement services, not individual APIs. Customers choose a listed service Y;
they do not add catalog entries. A stable serviceId identifies Y separately
from its payment recipient A. Both are fixed channel terms, so independently
catalogued services can share a treasury address. A can initially be an ordinary
address; making it a contract requires a concrete purpose.

One E can hold many customer/service channels. A customer's backing for Y is
exclusive of channels with another service Yprime. Within Y's channel, however,
API-1 and API-2 purchases share that backing. E needs neither individual API
catalog entries nor per-API allocations. Y is the settlement counterpart E
pays at A; API credits are Y's accounting obligations, not claims enforceable
against E. APIs need no mutual trust, but depend on Y to avoid overcommitting
the shared balance and to honor its credits. A malicious Y can misaccount
credits; APIs bear that settlement-service risk.

The admin may add its own services without gaining access to other channels'
funds. Catalog removal blocks only new channels for that service. Existing
valid claims and withdrawal rights survive removal and cannot depend on the
catalog's current enabled flag. Payer, serviceId, recipient, asset,
authorization terms and claim-period duration are fixed at channel opening;
admin catalog control cannot redirect balances or suspend valid claims.

What E offers a channel
-----------------------

- Open a channel with fixed terms and dedicated customer/Y backing, fund it
  and top it up. Direct channel funding is the proposed starting point; an E
  free balance later allocated to channels is an alternative, not selected.
  Payments use native currency, with amounts in native base units; this does
  not select a deployment chain. Detailed funding mechanics remain undecided.
- Collect cumulative authorizations. Y submits the highest valid cumulative
  amount it holds, regardless of voucher arrival order. If C is authorized
  and P is already paid, C <= P produces no further payment. For C > P, a
  valid claim pays C - P to A and advances P to C, subject to backing and
  channel validity/deadline rules. Onward payouts to APIs remain unspecified.
- Exit through open -> closing -> closed. The customer requests unilateral
  closing by transaction. E's transition starts the claim period, whose
  duration was fixed at opening and cannot change retroactively. Valid signed
  vouchers remain claimable during that period; afterwards the customer can
  withdraw the unclaimed remainder and close. The duration and exact
  transition rules remain open. Cooperative early close/refund rules are
  also undecided.
- Expose channel state and events for funding, cumulative collection and
  closing/withdrawal progress. E sees confirmed funding and collection, not
  every outstanding signed voucher or offchain reservation. Its remaining
  balance alone is therefore not the customer's spendable amount.

Replay protection must last across closure and any reuse: paid accounting
cannot reset while old vouchers still match the same identity. Fresh
identifiers/epochs versus retained monotonic accounting remains a design choice.

What the customer does
----------------------

The customer discovers settlement-service terms in E's catalog, chooses Y,
opens/funds a channel and tops it up as needed. The customer can inspect their
channels, deposits, collected amounts and closing status. To show spendable
funds, the customer and Y must also retain outstanding authorizations and
reservations offchain; E cannot reconstruct them from settled state.

The funding transaction authorizes the deposit and channel terms. API purchases
require separate offchain authorization; depositing does not authorize
arbitrary charges. The customer signs cumulative vouchers bound to the selected
chain, E deployment and channel identity/fixed terms, including serviceId and A.
The signing format and invoice authentication remain undecided. Dollar
illustrations such as $50 funding or a $0.50 purchase do not define a
native-token exchange rate. The customer can initiate the unilateral exit
above without relying on Y's cooperation.

Concurrent purchases and settlement-service duties
-------------------------------------------------

Purchases for different APIs through Y must run concurrently within one
channel. Y verifies the customer's authorization, channel and invoice/request
association, checks confirmed backing against tracked obligations, and must
atomically account for reservations, debits and API credits without
overcommitting funds. Y can briefly order ledger updates without serializing
all API purchases. The exact cumulative authorization, reservation and retry
protocol remains open.

A higher voucher authorizes its entire unpaid cumulative delta, including
amounts for pending or abandoned purchases if included in that total. The
protocol must define safe inclusion and reconciliation, voucher ordering and
abandonment handling. Independent per-request claims for the full cumulative
amount would pay repeatedly for the same authorization.

Y durably records accepted authorization and purchase status before API
provisioning proceeds. Y and the API need an idempotent, recoverable workflow:
retries recover the same purchase and recorded provisioning outcome rather
than silently creating another charge or resource. Globally exactly-once
external effects are not promised. Neither a voucher nor a purchase hash
proves correct delivery; recovery and refund rules still need design.

Y stops accepting new purchases when it observes closing under its confirmation
policy. The claim period starts at E's transition, so observation lag reduces
Y's response time. Y must monitor closing, track outstanding obligations and
claim in time, with explicit confirmation/reorg policies. Valid signatures
and visible backing alone are not an unconditional collection guarantee.
This design gives E no proof of API acceptance, delivery or signature creation
time. E enforces customer/Y settlement rules, not API delivery or Y's
liabilities to APIs.

Decisions still needed
---------------------

Settle funding mechanics; signer identity/key lifecycle;
invoice schema/authentication and request binding; the concurrent cumulative
authorization/reservation/retry protocol; claim-period length and exact closing
rules; cooperative close/refunds; reuse identifiers/accounting; provisioning
recovery; and Y's onward API payout arrangements. The selected counterpart,
parallel-purchase capability and fixed channel protections above remain the
design boundaries while these mechanisms are resolved.

Contract precedents and related patterns
---------------------------------------

Pinned source/specification snapshots and their provenance are under
dapps/pay-to-play/references/service-payment-channels/. These are research
material, not installed dependencies or executable fixtures; third-party
imports are not fully vendored. provenance.json records exact upstream commits
and byte hashes.

E's core is a unidirectional payment-channel kernel: customer authorization
lets one settlement counterpart collect from dedicated backing. Evaluate
existing implementations of that shape before writing a new kernel. Map the
payee role to Y, with A as its payment destination. This design additionally
requires the admin's Y catalog, serviceId distinct from payout address, a
claim-period duration fixed per channel, and CAM workflows. These precedents
do not choose a dependency, deployment chain or license, or establish safety
or suitability for this design.

AbstractStreamChannel is an ERC-20 precedent, while this design selects native
currency. Its operations include open, topUp, settle, requestClose, withdraw,
close and getChannel. Its EIP-712
cumulative vouchers let the payee call settle to collect the unpaid delta
without closing the channel. Its channel identity binds payer, payee, token,
salt, authorized signer, contract address and chain ID. It has a fixed
15-minute CLOSE_GRACE_PERIOD, not a per-channel duration, and no service
catalog or separate stable serviceId. This is a source-level precedent, not
a security assessment or an assertion of audit status.
https://github.com/Abstract-Foundation/abstract-packages/blob/main/packages/contracts/src/AbstractStreamChannel.sol

The historical RaidenMicroTransferChannels (microRaiden) design targets API
and content micropayments using deposits, signed cumulative balance proofs,
top-ups, cooperative close and unilateral close with a challenge period.
Settlement is primarily at channel closure rather than periodic delta claims
while open. Its repository was archived on 2022-05-13; it is a historical
design reference, not a selected dependency.
https://microraiden.readthedocs.io/en/latest/introduction/introduction.html
https://github.com/raiden-network/microraiden

x402's EVM batch-settlement specification describes prefunded channels,
cumulative vouchers and periodic claims, separating claim accounting from
recipient transfer. Its signed maxClaimableAmount is a ceiling: the server
tracks actual charges within it. That requires trust in charge accounting and
differs from this note's proposed exact cumulative amount C paid through delta
claims. Its receiver is a useful settlement-counterpart analogy; the full
protocol has not been adopted here.
https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/batch-settlement/scheme_batch_settlement_evm.md

Raiden's UserDeposit/OneToN design accepts competition among service payments
for shared backing and uses delayed withdrawal to limit risk. Shared-credit
risk is comparable at Y's accounting layer here; this does not imply unrelated
settlement services can drain the same E channel. Neither pattern guarantees
all API liabilities will be paid.
https://github.com/raiden-network/spec/blob/master/service_contracts.rst
