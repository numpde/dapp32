Service payment channels
========================

Design record, 2026-10-15. This specifies the selected minimal design, not an
implemented contract. Customers directly fund native-currency positions in
channels for settlement services. Offchain vouchers authorize cumulative
payments; API work need not wait for onchain collection. No deployment chain
or arbitrary default waiting period has been selected.

Actors and authority
--------------------

E is the deposit/settlement contract. D is its deployer/admin. C is the customer
and agent: C's account signs, with no separate human-owner/agent-key role.
Y is a channel's consumer/settlement service. A is its current payment recipient,
a wallet or contract. R is any submitter/relayer; submitting gives R no right
to choose where customer funds go.

D defines each channel's stable identity/serviceId, consumer Y and irreversible
claim-period duration. D may change A; existing vouchers always pay current A,
not a historical recipient. This redirection power is an explicit trust
assumption. D cannot change the wait, consumer, identity, customer balances or
paid totals, or fabricate C's authorization. The directory is permanent and
add-only, refining the earlier catalog-removal proposal; no disable/deletion
machinery is selected and existing rights remain intact.

A channel definition is separate from each C's position. Backing for Y and
Yprime is separate. APIs using Y share that position's backing and trust Y's
ledger and onward payouts; their credits are not claims against E. E needs no
per-API catalog or allocations. Reserved null channel 0 has no consumer and
uses the same funding, balance, movement and waiting rules, with an explicitly
admin-chosen immutable period through ordinary definition/initialization, not
an automatic zero-time bypass. Voucher collection requires a consumer, so the
consumerless null channel cannot pay Y vouchers.

One balance, two ways to consume it
----------------------------------

Each customer/channel position needs its available balance, lifetime paid total
for Y vouchers, and an optional withdrawal request containing only remaining
amount and ready time. These are logical facts, not a selected storage layout.
Funding/top-ups credit the balance in native base units. Internal movements
debit one of C's positions and credit another. No extra free-balance ledger,
withdrawal lifetime counter, debt ledger, epoch or status enum is needed.

Voucher collection uses standard EIP-712 authorization binding C, the channel
and cumulative total, with chain ID and E deployment in the signing domain.
It does not bind A. Use the existing OpenZeppelin SignatureChecker support for
EOA and ERC-1271 customer accounts, not custom cryptography or cached signature
validity. Contract-wallet authorization must be valid when collection executes.

Anyone may submit a valid voucher. If its authorized total exceeds the lifetime
paid total, pay min(authorized total - paid total, current balance) to current
A. Advance paid only by the amount transferred. Valid repeated/older vouchers
succeed without payment; zero payments change no state. Paid never resets.
Vouchers have no protocol expiry: an old unpaid voucher can collect after a
withdrawal and later funding, subject to current signature validity. There is
no closing/reopening or epoch mechanism.

Withdrawals use an onchain waiting entitlement instead of a second signed
voucher. The following rules also govern the source of an internal movement:

- Anyone may request an amount for C. If a request exists, ordinary request is
  a no-op before checking the new amount, even when C retries. Otherwise require
  0 < requested amount <= current balance, store that remaining amount, and
  set ready time to the request timestamp plus the channel's fixed period.
- Only C may explicitly set/renew: it creates or replaces a request, requires
  a positive amount no greater than current balance, and starts a full new
  wait. No cancellation operation is selected.
- Execution is allowed at timestamp >= ready time. Anyone may withdraw to C;
  R cannot choose another recipient or an internal destination. Only C may
  instead move the released funds to another of its own channel positions.
  Reject source = destination as a meaningless movement.
- Pay/move min(remaining amount, current balance). Subtract the actual amount
  from both. A partial execution preserves the unpaid remainder at the same
  ready time, so later funding can satisfy it without another wait. Clear the
  request at zero remaining amount. A zero payout changes nothing.

Collections reduce the shared balance, not the requested remaining amount.
Top-ups change neither amount nor ready time. A mature withdrawal and a Y
collection have no hard priority: transaction order determines which consumes
available funds first. A withdrawal request caps an entitlement; it does not
reserve a separate pot of coins. Native payments and accounting must be atomic:
a reverting recipient rolls back the operation, and callbacks cannot consume
the same entitlement twice.

What C and Y see and do
----------------------

C chooses a channel, directly funds/top-ups its position, signs vouchers and
can inspect balance, paid total and withdrawal progress. Funding alone does
not authorize charges. Dollar illustrations such as $50 funding or a $0.50
purchase do not define a native-token exchange rate. E exposes state/events
for these onchain facts, not all signed vouchers, purchase reservations or
API liabilities. C and Y must retain that offchain history to show spendable
funds and outstanding authorization accurately.

Y maintains one durable cumulative authorization stream per C/channel. Brief
atomic acceptance assigns purchase/request IDs and reconciles authorization,
backing and API credits; the actual API work can run concurrently. Retries must
recover the same purchase and recorded result instead of silently charging or
provisioning twice. Exact invoice, sequencing and recovery mechanisms are
outside this core note, not prerequisites for defining E's accounting.

Because anyone can initiate a withdrawal, it does not mean C is closing the
channel or canceling purchases. Y must account for pending/mature withdrawals
when accepting work; a blanket stop on every request would let strangers
interrupt service. Y must monitor balance, current payee and requests under its
confirmation policy. Observation lag reduces time before withdrawal becomes
executable. Signed authorization does not guarantee backing remains until Y
collects, prove delivery or settle Y's liabilities to APIs. Delivery refunds
and Y's onward API payouts remain outside E's core rules; no globally
exactly-once external effect is promised.

Worked cases and future proof obligations
----------------------------------------

With balance 30, paid 0 and a voucher for total 100, collection pays 30 and
records paid 30. Funding another 20 lets the same voucher collect 20, recording
paid 50. Repeating any valid total <= 50 succeeds with no payment. Withdrawing
funds does not forgive the unpaid authorization.

With balance 100 and a request for 80, a collection of 60 leaves balance 40
and requested remainder 80. At maturity withdrawal pays 40, leaving a mature
remainder 40. Funding 50 allows another withdrawal of 40, leaving balance 10.
No top-up resets the deadline or increases the entitlement. C can instead
explicitly renew within the current balance, restarting the full wait.

Future contract tests must establish:
- Voucher partial collection, idempotent retry and unpaid collection after
  withdrawal/redeposit; lifetime paid changes only by actual voucher payments.
- Signature replay rejection across chains, E deployments, customers and
  channels, with contract-wallet validity checked at execution.
- Rejection before ready time and acceptance exactly at it; partial withdrawal
  keeps maturity, full payment clears, and zero payout preserves the request.
- Ordinary duplicate requests are no-ops, including invalid replacement amounts;
  C's explicit set/renew works with or without an existing request, validates
  its amount and starts a full new wait; other callers cannot use it.
- Top-ups and voucher payments leave request amount/deadline unchanged until
  actual withdrawal/movement consumes it; null positions follow the same rules.
- Current A receives old vouchers after D changes A; unprivileged updates and
  changes to fixed channel terms or customer accounting fail.
- Both transaction orders for mature withdrawal versus collection; anyone's
  withdrawal pays only C, only C routes internal moves, and source = destination
  is rejected.
- Funding/movement/payment conservation, no cross-position overdraft, and full
  rollback on reverting native calls or attempted callback double consumption.

Contract precedents and related patterns
---------------------------------------

Pinned source/specification snapshots and their provenance are under
dapps/pay-to-play/references/service-payment-channels/. These are research
material, not installed dependencies or executable fixtures; third-party
imports are not fully vendored. provenance.json records exact upstream commits
and byte hashes.

E's core is a unidirectional payment-channel kernel: customer authorization
lets one settlement counterpart collect from dedicated backing. Evaluate
existing implementations of that shape before writing a new kernel. Map the
payee role to Y, with A as its payment destination. This design additionally
requires D's channel directory, serviceId distinct from payout address, a
claim-period duration fixed per channel, and CAM workflows. These precedents
do not choose a dependency, deployment chain or license, or establish safety
or suitability for this design.

AbstractStreamChannel is an ERC-20 precedent, while this design selects native
currency. Its operations include open, topUp, settle, requestClose, withdraw,
close and getChannel. Its EIP-712 cumulative vouchers let the payee call
settle to collect the unpaid delta
without closing the channel. Its channel identity binds payer, payee, token,
salt, authorized signer, contract address and chain ID. It has a fixed
15-minute CLOSE_GRACE_PERIOD, not a per-channel duration, and no service
catalog or separate stable serviceId. This is a source-level precedent, not
a security assessment or an assertion of audit status.
https://github.com/Abstract-Foundation/abstract-packages/blob/main/packages/contracts/src/AbstractStreamChannel.sol

The historical RaidenMicroTransferChannels (microRaiden) design targets API
and content micropayments using deposits, signed cumulative balance proofs,
top-ups, cooperative close and unilateral close with a challenge period.
Its receiver can also withdraw cumulative balance-proof deltas while the
channel is open. Its repository was archived on 2022-05-13; it is a historical
design reference, not a selected dependency.
https://microraiden.readthedocs.io/en/latest/introduction/introduction.html
https://github.com/raiden-network/microraiden

x402's EVM batch-settlement specification describes prefunded channels,
cumulative vouchers and periodic claims, separating claim accounting from
recipient transfer. Its signed maxClaimableAmount is a ceiling: the server
tracks actual charges within it. That requires trust in charge accounting and
differs from this note's cumulative entitlement, collectible in partial
deltas as backing permits. Its receiver is a useful settlement-counterpart
analogy; the full protocol has not been adopted here.
https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/batch-settlement/scheme_batch_settlement_evm.md

The pinned x402 finalizeWithdraw and Raiden UserDeposit withdrawHelper cap
withdrawal by the requested amount and current available balance, then clear
the request even after partial payment. Retaining a mature unpaid remainder
is this design's explicit difference. AbstractStreamChannel cancels pending
closing on top-up, whereas this design preserves the withdrawal deadline.

Raiden's UserDeposit/OneToN design accepts competition among service payments
for shared backing and uses delayed withdrawal to limit risk. Shared-credit
risk is comparable at Y's accounting layer here; this does not imply unrelated
settlement services can drain the same E channel. Neither pattern guarantees
all API liabilities will be paid.
https://github.com/raiden-network/spec/blob/master/service_contracts.rst
