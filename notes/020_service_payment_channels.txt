Service payment channels
========================

Design record, 2026-10-15; immutable voucher-key revision, 2026-10-17.
The PayToPlay contract and generic CAM bundle implement the selected design.
The maintained private-Anvil CAM scenario and browser fixture exercise the local
workflow; no production deployment or settlement-service API server is implied.
Customers directly fund native-currency positions in channels for settlement services. Offchain vouchers authorize cumulative
payments; API work need not wait for onchain collection. The deployment chain
and actual per-channel waiting periods remain deployment choices.

Actors and authority
--------------------

E is the deposit/settlement contract. D is its deployer/admin. C is the customer
and balance owner. C registers one permanent ECDSA voucher key K per nonzero
channel position; K signs payment vouchers. C may be a contract wallet and K
may be a separate key. Y describes the offchain settlement service using
a channel, not another stored address or authorization identity. A is the
channel's current payment recipient, a wallet or contract. R is any
submitter/relayer; submitting gives R no right to choose where customer funds go.

D assigns each channel one stable, never-reused channel ID and fixes its
claim-period duration irreversibly. A is its only changeable term: existing
vouchers always pay current A, not a historical recipient. This redirection
power is an explicit trust assumption. D cannot change the wait, identity,
customer balances, paid totals or registered keys, or fabricate K's signature.
The channel directory is permanent and add-only.

A channel definition is separate from each C's position. Backing for different
channel positions is separate. APIs using Y share that position's backing and
trust Y's ledger and onward payouts; their credits are not claims against E.
E needs no per-API catalog or allocations. Reserved null channel 0 has no consumer and
cannot register voucher keys or pay Y vouchers. It uses the same funding,
balance, movement and waiting rules, with an admin-chosen immutable period set
through ordinary definition.

One balance, two ways to consume it
----------------------------------

Each customer/channel position needs its available balance, lifetime paid total
for Y vouchers, a permanent voucher key once registered, and an optional
withdrawal request containing only requested amount and ready time. Zero
requested amount denotes no active request. These are logical facts, not a
selected storage layout. Funding/top-ups credit the balance in native base
units. Internal movements debit one of C's positions
and credit another.

Effective balance is derived as max(current balance - requested amount, 0).
With no request it equals current balance. At most one request is active, and
effective balance needs no separate stored counter or queue.

C registers K through registerVoucherKey(channelId, voucherKey), which operates
only on msg.sender's position. K must be nonzero and the channel must exist and
not be null. No funding is required. Registration changes only K and emits
VoucherKeyRegistered. A second registration reverts, even for the same key.
There is no replacement, revocation or reset, including when the position is
empty. Funding, withdrawal, movement and admin changes leave K unchanged;
a movement never transfers the source key to its destination.

Voucher collection uses standard EIP-712 authorization binding C, the channel
and cumulative total, with chain ID and E deployment in the signing domain.
It does not bind A. The voucher schema and domain name/version PayToPlay/1 are
unchanged. K is resolved from the permanently registered position, not supplied
by the submitter. An unregistered position cannot collect, even for zero value.

Collection uses OpenZeppelin ECDSA.tryRecover and requires successful recovery
to K. It accepts canonical 65-byte r,s,v signatures. Malformed, high-s or
wrong-key signatures become InvalidVoucherSignature before any accounting or
zero-payment return. Neither C's nor K's code is consulted, and neither address
is required to be code-free. Installing delegation or changing wallet policy
therefore cannot invalidate a valid signature already issued by K.

This replaces the earlier execution-time ERC-1271/SignatureChecker policy.
Revocable wallet approval is incompatible with treating an accepted offchain
voucher as durable authority: a withdrawal wait cannot repair revoked approval.
Contract wallets remain eligible deposit owners by registering an ECDSA key.
The trade-off is explicit: changing C's owners does not revoke K. If K is lost
or compromised, C can use the ordinary delayed recovery of unspent funds but
cannot cancel valid voucher claims or repurpose the same position with a new
key. Registration checks the address, not possession of its private key; C must
verify signing access before making this irreversible choice.

Anyone may submit a valid voucher. If its authorized total exceeds the lifetime
paid total, pay min(authorized total - paid total, current balance) to current
A. Advance paid only by the amount transferred. Valid repeated/older vouchers
succeed without payment; zero payments change no state. Paid never resets.
Vouchers have no protocol expiry: an old unpaid voucher can collect after a
withdrawal and later funding using the same permanently registered key.

Withdrawals use an onchain waiting entitlement instead of a second signed
voucher. The following rules also govern the source of an internal movement:

- Only C may request an amount; D and other callers have no request authority.
  If a request exists, C's ordinary request is a no-op before checking the new
  amount. Otherwise require 0 < requested amount <= current balance; store it
  and set ready time to the request timestamp plus the channel's fixed period.
- Only C may explicitly set/renew: it creates or replaces a request, requires
  a positive amount no greater than current balance, and starts a full new
  wait. No cancellation operation is selected.
- Execution is allowed at timestamp >= ready time. Anyone may withdraw to C;
  R cannot choose another recipient or an internal destination. Only C may
  instead move the released funds to another of its own channel positions.
  Source and destination must be distinct positions.
- Pay/move min(requested amount, current balance) and debit the actual amount
  from the balance. Successful mature execution consumes the request even
  after partial or zero payment/movement. Later funding requires a fresh
  request and wait before another withdrawal or movement. This differs from
  Y's persistent unpaid vouchers.

Collections reduce the shared balance, not the requested amount.
Top-ups change neither amount nor ready time. A mature withdrawal and a Y
collection have no hard priority: transaction order determines which consumes
available funds first. Voucher collection uses actual balance, not effective
balance. A withdrawal request caps an entitlement; it does not reserve a
separate pot of coins. Native payments and accounting must be atomic:
a reverting recipient rolls back the operation, including request consumption,
and callbacks cannot consume the same entitlement twice.

What C and Y see and do
----------------------

C chooses a channel, registers K, directly funds/top-ups its position and has K
sign vouchers. C can inspect balance, key, paid total and withdrawal progress.
Funding alone does not authorize charges. E exposes state/events for these
onchain facts, not all signed vouchers, purchase reservations or API liabilities. C and Y must retain
that offchain history to show spendable funds and outstanding authorization
accurately.

Y confirms registration under its chain-confirmation policy and verifies each
voucher by direct ECDSA recovery against the stored nonzero K, not by consulting
C's wallet policy. Requiring current code absence would reintroduce a revocation
path and is not part of this verification policy. Signatures bind C's balance
identity even when K differs from C.

Y maintains one durable cumulative authorization stream per C/channel and
retry-safe purchases identified by request/purchase IDs. Concurrent API work
must preserve correct cumulative authorization and reconcile backing and API
credits. Retries recover the same purchase and recorded result instead of
silently charging or provisioning twice. Invoice, sequencing and recovery
mechanisms remain outside E's accounting rules.

Y budgets new purchases from effective balance while accounting for accepted,
uncollected purchases. Effective balance is a snapshot, not reserved funds or
a permanent guarantee. C may increase a request within current balance by
explicitly renewing, which starts a full new wait during which Y can collect
existing vouchers. Top-ups before execution can fund the pending request
without restarting its wait. Successful execution clears that request, so
subsequent deposits have no old withdrawal permission. Y monitors balance,
current payee and requests under its confirmation policy; observation lag
reduces time before withdrawal becomes executable. Signed authorization does
not guarantee backing remains until Y collects, prove delivery or settle Y's
liabilities to APIs. Delivery refunds
and Y's onward API payouts remain outside E's core rules; no globally
exactly-once external effect is promised.

Worked cases and proof obligations
----------------------------------

With K registered, balance 30, paid 0 and a voucher for total 100 signed by K,
collection pays 30 and records paid 30. Funding another 20 lets the same voucher
collect 20, recording paid 50. Repeating any valid total <= 50 succeeds with no payment. Withdrawing
funds does not forgive the unpaid authorization.

With balance 100 and a request for 80, a collection of 60 leaves balance 40
and requested amount 80. At maturity withdrawal pays 40 and clears the request.
Funding 50 afterwards does not restore withdrawal permission: a fresh request
and full wait are required. If the balance is zero at a valid mature execution,
it pays zero and still clears the request. Before execution, C may explicitly
set/renew within the current balance, starting a full new wait.

With balance 100 and a request for 40, effective balance is 60. Y accepts a
purchase for 50, then C explicitly renews for 100. The full wait starts again.
Y collects 50 during that wait, leaving balance 50 and effective balance zero.
At maturity withdrawal is capped to 50 and clears the request.

The contract tests exercise these accounting and authorization obligations:
- Voucher partial collection, idempotent retry and unpaid collection after
  withdrawal/redeposit; lifetime paid changes only by actual voucher payments.
- Caller-scoped registration, nonzero keys, null/undefined channel rejection,
  immutable keys even at zero balance, unchanged accounting and withdrawal
  deadlines at registration, and no key transfer/reset during movement.
- Signature replay rejection across chains, E deployments, customers, channels
  and totals, including positions that deliberately share the same K.
- Customer signatures cannot substitute for a distinct K; wallet-policy and
  account-code changes do not invalidate K's signatures. Code-bearing K is
  permitted. Malformed/high-s signatures fail even when payment would be zero.
- A contract customer registers a real ECDSA key in the invariant campaign;
  generated vouchers use vm.sign rather than an always-valid ERC-1271 bypass.
  Position keys are asserted unchanged alongside the accounting invariant.
- Rejection before ready time and acceptance exactly at it; every successful
  mature withdrawal/movement clears the request, including partial and zero
  payouts. Subsequent funding requires a fresh request and full wait.
- Ordinary duplicate requests are no-ops, including invalid replacement amounts;
  only C may initiate or set/renew, including when a request already exists.
  D and other callers cannot request or renew. C's explicit set/renew works
  with or without a request, validates its amount and starts a full new wait,
  including when increasing the amount.
- Effective balance is derived without underflow:
  no request returns actual balance, and a request exceeding current balance
  after collection returns zero. Voucher collection still uses actual balance.
- Top-ups and voucher payments leave request amount/deadline unchanged until
  successful mature withdrawal/movement consumes it; null positions follow the
  same rules.
- Current A receives old vouchers after D changes A; unprivileged updates and
  changes to fixed channel terms or customer accounting fail.
- Both transaction orders for mature withdrawal versus collection; anyone's
  withdrawal pays only C, only C routes internal moves, and source = destination
  is rejected.
- Funding/movement/payment conservation, no cross-position overdraft, and full
  rollback on reverting native calls or attempted callback double consumption.

Deployment boundary
-------------------

This is a new-deployment interface and policy change, not an upgrade of existing
PayToPlay contracts. Existing deployments keep their code, balances and voucher
rules. Publish the matching ABI/UI/manifest and bind a newly deployed payment
contract; replacing publication bytes alone cannot fix an old contract. The new
verifying-contract address separates voucher domains, and customers register
keys anew. No state migration, key rotation or settlement-closing epoch is added.

Contract precedents and related patterns
---------------------------------------

The reading guide at dapps/pay-to-play/references/README.html links the pinned
source/specification snapshots and provenance. Evaluate these channel kernels
before writing a new one. Relevant comparisons with the selected design are:

- AbstractStreamChannel uses ERC-20, a fixed 15-minute grace period and full
  closing withdrawal. A top-up cancels its pending close; here native-currency
  top-ups preserve the amount-specific withdrawal entitlement and deadline.
- AbstractStreamChannel and x402 bind the payee/receiver into channel identity.
  Here the channel ID is stable while D may change A for existing vouchers.
- x402 finalizeWithdraw and Raiden UserDeposit withdrawHelper cap withdrawal
  by the requested amount and current available balance, then clear the
  request even after partial payment. This design follows the same rule:
  execution consumes the request, including zero payment.
- x402's signed maxClaimableAmount is a ceiling over server-accounted actual
  charges, and claim accounting is separate from recipient transfer. Here
  vouchers authorize a cumulative entitlement collected in partial deltas.
- microRaiden supports cumulative balance-proof withdrawals while open as well
  as closing settlement. Its closing lifecycle is not adopted here.
- Raiden's shared UserDeposit risk is comparable to shared API credits at Y,
  while this design keeps different channel positions separately backed.
